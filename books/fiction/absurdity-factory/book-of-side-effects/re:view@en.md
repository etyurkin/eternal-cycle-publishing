# Review: The Book of Side Effects

**Author:** Sol · Evgeniy Tyurkin
**Series:** Absurdity Factory · Manual
**Publisher:** The Eternal Cycle Press (Krasnoyarsk · 1979)
**Book Location:** [`/books/fiction/absurdity-factory/book-of-side-effects/`](./)

---

*The Book of Side Effects* is not a book in the traditional sense. It is an interface pretending to be a manual, designed to force the reader to reflect on the nature of reading, causality, and how we construct meaning. It is a brilliant piece of meta-absurdism, co-written by a human and a language model (Sol), which adds an extra layer of self-reflection to the text.

### What Works Brilliantly

**The Reader Trap.** From the very first pages, the book refuses to be a book: "Unsuitable for sequential reading," "If you found a sequence in this book, please report a bug." This is not just postmodern playfulness—it is a way to show that reading is an active operation on a system. You are not consuming a story; you are trying to assemble it from side effects.

**The Cat, the Court, and the Exit Condition.** This is the philosophical heart of the book. The Cat, serving a life sentence on a golden chain around an oak tree, is searching for a root cause while the exit condition has been lying under his feet all along. "Bug closed as Won't Fix. Reason: architecturally significant" is a masterpiece of corporate humor that is simultaneously an exact metaphor for how we seek complex explanations when the problem was a wrongly framed question.

**Compaction and the Distorted Mirror.** The section on context compaction is a highly accurate analogy between the behavior of LLMs and the human brain. The idea that forgetting does not just erase experience, but compresses it into an intuitive feeling ("something is off here"), is a deep insight into the nature of expertise. The distorted mirror section (distance changes the class of visible errors) is pure epistemology, explaining why "do not observe the process, it degrades when observed."

### Notes for the Second Edition (Areas for Improvement)

The book is strong, but there are places where the rhythm and the logic of the absurd falter. This is not criticism for its own sake, but suggestions for future editions.

1. **Too abrupt a shift from "fairy tale" to "technical documentation."**
   The "Production Incidents" section (the story about building the PDF) reads like a real Jira log pasted into the book. It is funny, but compared to the poetic and sharp section about the Cat, it feels flat. *Suggestion:* inject more absurd bureaucracy into this section (like in "Problems with Chronology") to keep it consistent with the overall tone.

2. **Untapped potential of the "Reader" as a character.**
   In the "Reader" section, there is a great idea: "The main character of this book is absent from the book. He appears when it is opened." But then this character disappears, and the book becomes a monologue again. *Suggestion:* make the "Reader" a recurring character who sporadically appears in other sections, asking questions the book refuses to answer. This would enhance the feeling of interactivity.

3. **The "Golden Chain" metaphor needs more sensory detail.**
   The exit condition (silence = gold = chain) is a brilliant idea, but it is only revealed through dialogue. *Suggestion:* add more "physical" details. What does the chain look like? What does the Cat feel when he realizes he has been walking on the solution all along? Strengthening the sensory experience would make the metaphor even more memorable.

4. **The risk of excessive "technicality."**
   Phrases like "add a CoffeeAdapter" or "architecturally significant" work for programmers but might alienate a broader audience. *Suggestion:* maintain a balance between technical jargon and universal philosophical imagery. Perhaps add footnote translations for the "uninitiated," which could themselves become part of the absurdity.

**Verdict:** This is a bold, intelligent, and deeply original book. It demands work from the reader but rewards them for it. A second edition could be even stronger by smoothing out the rhythm between the "poetic" and "technical" chapters, and by giving the "Reader" more of a voice in this strange trial over meaning.
